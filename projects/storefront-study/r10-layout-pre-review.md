# R10 shop circulation pre-review

Independent architectural review of the existing production planners and the new photo-informed AI concepts. The concepts are layout hypotheses; their drawn plans are not dimensional evidence. This review concerns plausible visual furnishing and circulation, not collision-aware gameplay or building-code certification.

## Required repairs

- The roast shop's current side entry feeds directly into a prep bench, stool, bucket and floor storage. Adding tables there would mix seated customers with the cook's working area. In the 4.8 m study bay, reserve a roughly 2.05 m side strip: about 1.15 m for tables and occupied stools, plus a continuous 0.9 m customer aisle. Shorten the front display/cashier counter accordingly and move side preparation to the kitchen side.
- Preserve a real staff crossover behind the front counter. A partition starting at depth 1.0 m would leave as little as 0.19 m behind the deeper counter placement. Start it at least 0.75 m behind the counter's rear, or provide an explicit opening of that width. Keep customers' route clear of the chopping station and rear stove.
- Two proposed dining modules occupy depths 1.2–2.6 m and 2.85–4.25 m. Their count must come from the available depth before the rear kitchen, rather than always drawing two at the threshold room size. At 5.2 m depth, a kitchen cutoff at depth minus 1.1 m permits only the first module; two fit from approximately 5.4 m.
- The provisions side counter currently hugs the wall, leaving no credible clerk side. Stand it off the wall in sufficiently wide rooms, with a connected roughly 0.8 m staff strip and at least 0.9 m customer lane on its other side. Put the register, bagging space and ledger on this counter rather than adding them to stock shelves.
- Retain all three provisions layout families where they fit. Introduce explicit staff and customer reservations beside the existing fixture plan, and shorten or omit the island, racks and bulk displays that violate them. Preserve cross-passages before and behind the island. The elevated right rack counts as an obstruction where its bottom is only 1.35 m high.
- The smallest provisions layout currently lets crates almost close the gap beside the front counter. Counter-service shops still require staff entry: omit or relocate threshold crates when a side opening of approximately 0.7 m cannot remain. A stool may occupy a clerk's seat location but must not obstruct the access strip.
- The produce shop's roughly 0.82 m right slot should be closer to 0.95 m where width permits. Its checkout can be the destination at the back of that lane, facing the customer with the cashier behind. Reserve a staff strip behind the tiered produce bank; relocate the melon pallet or empty crates that would otherwise occupy it. A register on the front tiers would be inaccessible set dressing.

## Adaptation by size

Deep wide rooms can support a low central provisions island or a small eat-in area. Narrow/shallow rooms should reduce fixture count and use counter service, with no dining furniture. Do not scale every arrangement down uniformly until circulation disappears. Thick wood boards and deeper decks must be included in measured fixture envelopes.

Inspect a roof-off plan and ordinary interior views of each revised shop, including the occupied table/stool envelopes and cashier work side. Verify continuous routes from the threshold to checkout, rear browsing or dining, and staff work areas as appropriate. Check both reflected layouts, small rooms, and the dimensions immediately around seating/island eligibility thresholds. Check corner turns and vertical obstruction ranges as well as individual clear widths. Preserve physical collision hashes; visual route clearance alone is not a playable-navigation claim.
