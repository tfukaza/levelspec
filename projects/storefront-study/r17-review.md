Status: the central-counter source attribution is substantially improved; the complete scene still does not match the concept-art brief. This is visual evidence, not a functional or navigation approval.

Inspected the full active reference `concepts/paintover-r13/roast-cutaway.png` first, then `../../generated/storefront-study/r17/roast-cutaway.png`, `roast.png`, `sources-combined.png`, and `source-comparison.png`. ## Lighting findings

1. **Display birds have isolated burned highlights rather than continuous amber form.** In the street view, four belly/flank patches appear orange-white while the other birds and intervening body surfaces remain dark brown. The fixture-only interior panel makes the problem unmistakable: near-black torsos punctuated by bright neck and side islands. The reference has readable amber bodies with small highlights across the row. Raise the actual modeled lamp heads to increase source-to-bird distance, broaden their overlapping coverage and reduce candela to avoid increasing the worktop's already strong brightness. Keep the sources at their modeled emitters. This is the highest-priority visual lighting improvement; global fill or exposure would also brighten the counter and would not resolve the distribution problem.

2. **Center worktop illumination now has a credible visible source.** Its bright band occurs in fixture-only lighting beneath the ceiling battens. It is no longer reasonable to call that illumination unexplained. However, the long pale empty top remains a stronger shape than the birds in the cutaway. The sparse worktop composition magnifies the apparent lighting problem. Keep useful clear chopping/prep space, but concentrate a believable arrangement of trays, stacked plates and serving vessels at the rear shelf and working end rather than evenly scattering clutter.

3. **Hood-wall spill is less dominant, but the room still has harsh direct-light-only transitions.** The backsplash pool is smaller and warmer than the prior image; the black ceiling and much of the dark shelving still contrast sharply with pale counters. This is consistent with a real-time rig lacking indirect bounce, not proof of a physically complete lighting solution. Prefer a bounded indirect-light approximation tied to the lit room surfaces, or an actual bake, over restoring invisible point-light fill.

The reported R17 pendant/front-glass intersection must be corrected before accepting its geometry. The proposed `counterY + .16` clearance change is not represented in these frozen captures and is not approved by this review.

## Remaining art gaps

- The concept's substantial sloped hood, ribbed intake, layered cooking controls and warm roaster hatch carry much of the back-wall detail. The model's thin black hood and plain cabinet stove leave a large bare tiled region. Give the hood a deeper sloped body and a readable intake before adding decorative clutter.
- The peninsula's raised storage is thin and sparsely populated. The reference shows a dense stack of plates, steamers, tins and useful service items in the same central volume. Increase occupied storage volume while retaining the actual passage and work clearances.
- The model floor reads as a flat dark grid; the reference has localized warm reflections and irregular handling deposits. Add restrained roughness and stain variation aligned with traffic and work zones, rather than making every tile glossy.
- Wall wear remains uniform small chips on a very dark dado, while the reference has broader broken paint, seams, deposits and concentrated marks at handling height. Use fewer larger localized worn regions and preserve variation within the green paint.

Shadows now ground the furniture, and the wall corruption is absent in inspected captures. Those improvements do not establish overall art acceptance. R17 remains a visibly sparse, harder-lit interpretation of the active concept.
