# Roast eatery: independent r13 art brief

Assessment: **changes required; r12 is not visually ready.**

Scope: independent environment-art and reference-fit assessment. I inspected the original concept and real photograph before the three current renders. I did not read earlier reviews, author explanations, or test results. This is themed invention, not an exact reconstruction. Dimensions proposed below are design estimates, not measurements from photographs or a clearance certification.

The concept uses a narrow left dining/circulation strip beside a continuous, densely stocked service spine. Its front glass cabinet holds a close rhythm of hanging birds; counters, shelves, ceiling fixtures and exterior services overlap in depth. The real photograph supports the underlying compression: tables, bodies, service equipment, overhead fans and ceiling equipment compete for a small volume. It does not establish the concept's exact plan. Both sources feel occupied vertically as well as horizontally.

R12 does contain recognizable tables, stools, a roast display, preparation furniture, woks, storage and enclosure. The basic front display/right-service and left-dining idea can work. Those facts do not offset the following conspicuous visual mismatches.

## Five biggest failures, ordered by impact

### 1. Empty floor and wall volume dominate the restaurant

**Evidence:** The interior view is dominated by a broad red floor lane, uninterrupted left-wall tiles and a large blank rear-left wall/corner. In the cutaway, two little tables sit against a long wall with open floor around them; the room is substantially more spacious than the concept's compressed bay. From outside, the open left half reads as a nearly empty hall.

**Correction:** Compress the envelope and bring the service spine closer to the dining edge. Make the customer route a deliberate narrow passage between occupied stools and counter, not a broad central floor feature. Reduce unused depth behind the second table. Use the rear-left corner for a shallow, believable vertical stack of takeaway stock or dining storage. Do not merely scatter crates across the empty floor. This requires layout changes before prop polish.

**Acceptance target:** In the same front and interior cameras, tables/stools, counter and stocked rear edge must overlap to produce a small working room. The broad uninterrupted red floor polygon and large empty rear-left patch should no longer be primary shapes. The cutaway must still expose a continuous customer route and a usable staff route, assessed separately from visual density.

### 2. The work area reads as disconnected furniture islands

**Evidence:** The cutaway shows a long central counter, a separate shorter right counter, the front display counter, and rear appliances with generous gaps. In the interior, the long service surface has isolated plate stacks and no convincing sequence of working zones. This produces a showroom arrangement of objects rather than an accumulated food-service workplace.

**Correction:** Connect the front-right display counter to a longitudinal service return. Put chopping, packing and payment at its front; plated food, bowls and utensils along its inner return. Place a single compact cooking zone at back-right beneath its hood, with the one sink adjacent along the right side. Right-wall shelving should directly serve that narrow staff lane. Use under-counter storage and high stock shelves instead of free-standing duplicate benches. Preserve a rear crossover for staff; the design must not trap the work zone behind an uninterrupted barrier.

**Acceptance target:** A roof-off capture should explain the service sequence at a glance: hanging roast/chop/pack at front, preparation/storage along the return, woks and sink behind. An eye-level capture must show useful objects grouped at those jobs, not bare tabletops with evenly spaced tokens.

### 3. The roast-window focal point is sparse and incorrectly proportioned visually

**Evidence:** In the exterior, three very large birds occupy only the right part of a wide glass assembly. Their mass overwhelms the nearby register, bowls and work surface, while substantial glass area is empty. The concept instead uses numerous smaller birds to create a dense horizontal band across the cabinet. This is a conspicuous identity mismatch, irrespective of the asset's absolute source dimensions.

**Correction:** Re-scale against the chopping block, plate stacks and counter height in the actual render. Use a close but not mechanical rhythm of roughly five to seven smaller birds, with modest silhouette/height/rotation variation and visible hooks on one coherent rail. Fit the cabinet tightly around the display and chopping station. Retain sufficient readable glazing and metal frame; avoid making the cabinet a bare open scaffold or dark opaque box.

**Acceptance target:** At delivery image size, the viewer should immediately read a stocked roast-meat cabinet behind glass. Individual birds should remain identifiable without becoming oversized mascots. Inspect a close capture for hooks, hanging clearance, glass readability and plausible counter scale.

### 4. Grime is uniform decoration instead of material history

**Evidence:** Nearly every interior wall is the same brown-beige tile field, with repeated dark marks and continuous staining; the red floor is a largely featureless dark plane. The concept's green-painted walls, tiled dado, worn timber, stainless surfaces and grubby joints create material hierarchy. R12's similarly dirty surfaces flatten the room into one brown box. The exterior's large mottled plaster strip compounds this sameness.

**Correction:** Establish distinct zones: worn green paint above a cream tiled dado on the dining wall; wipeable tile/metal backsplash at cooking and sink; scratched stainless worktops; older timber or painted service cabinetry; small-format worn floor tiles with a readable grid. Put concentrated grease under/around the hood, splash marks near the sink, hand wear near doors/counter edges and darker joints near the floor. Allow some cleaned work surfaces and pale tile patches so grime has contrast. Dressing the tables with grouped condiments/chopsticks and a few used bowls will make them read as dining stations rather than two bare display tables.

**Acceptance target:** Materials and wear should communicate separate functional zones in the unannotated interior view. No single all-over dirt treatment should erase those differences. Floor detail must be readable without becoming the visual subject.

### 5. The ceiling and street edge lack the dense service layer that sells the setting

**Evidence:** The exterior has a broad blank fascia above a fairly clean geometric awning, without the concept's crowded pipes, wiring and mechanical attachments. The interior has a smooth ceiling with large soft light patches, a coarse blade-like fan and sparse fixtures. The real photograph has a low, occupied ceiling and conspicuous wall fans; the concept's exterior is deeply framed by services and wear. R12 currently reads as a standalone retail box with an awning.

**Correction:** Tighten the fascia and build a few coherent service assemblies: bundled cable entering a box, vertical drain/utility pipes, a duct or compact AC unit, and a wall fan aimed into dining. Add readable ceiling panels or seams and fixtures mounted to them. Refine the fan silhouette or replace it with a modest wall fan if the ceiling fan cannot read convincingly. Keep the awning as a weathered silhouette with actual sag/edge irregularity, not a giant decorative strip. Use a restrained warm display light against cooler/neutral work light; preserve dark depth and contact shadows without turning the whole room yellow-brown.

**Acceptance target:** A normal exterior view should read as one compressed shop embedded in a heavily serviced Kowloon street edge. The inside view must show believable overhead occupation and attached equipment. A close-up should not reveal disconnected decorative pipes, unsupported fixtures or a visibly crude fan as dominant features.

## Recommended compact arrangement before production commitment

Retain the concept's basic asymmetry and rebuild it compactly. Suggested starting envelope: approximately **3.6–3.9 m internal width by 5.0–5.5 m depth**, adjusted after body-clearance inspection. This is a proportion study, not a required numeric target.

- Left side: two small wall-hugging tables, approximately 0.45–0.50 m deep, each with two compact stools arranged deliberately. Put useful condiments/chopsticks on both. The second table should not leave a large empty room behind it.
- Middle-left: one customer passage, approximately 0.85–0.95 m at its intended clear section, checked with stools in actual occupied positions. Avoid treating clearance numbers as visual approval or allowing furniture to erase the route.
- Middle-right: a roughly 0.55–0.65 m deep service spine extending from the front display toward the back, with dense but purposeful front chopping/payment and return preparation zones. Keep its rear end short of the back fixtures so staff have a crossover.
- Right side: a single staff working lane, approximately 0.85–0.95 m at the intended clear section, beside shallow storage/counter elements. Use vertical shelving rather than another isolated work island.
- Front-right: the glass roast cabinet across the service frontage, with smaller densely arranged birds, chopping block, trays, register at the customer-facing end and under-counter takeaway storage.
- Back-right: compact two-wok range/hood and exactly one sink nearby. Keep the sink's basin, faucet and usable approach visually legible. Stock the rear-left wall shallowly; do not invent a large empty rear room.
- Overhead: low, coherent ceiling with panel seams, a few practical fixtures, fan and services. Maintain real enclosure and expose it through a deliberate cutaway only for inspection.

The cross-section should feel almost fully allocated: dining furniture, customer passage, service counter, staff passage, right-wall storage. The objective is purposeful density, not random clutter or blocked access. Plan changes should be checked for actual physical clearance separately.

## Captures required before calling the revised shop ready

1. **Same exterior three-quarter view as r12**, full enclosure present, at a comparable normal delivery size. Must show the dense smaller-bird display, an occupied glimpse of dining, a coherent awning/door/service frame, and stronger depth hierarchy. This is the main visual acceptance image; a prettier crop may not conceal the whole-shop proportions.
2. **Entrance eye-level interior**, looking into dining and the service return. Must show tight but believable route allocation, stocked working surfaces, materially distinct zones, occupied ceiling, and no dominant empty rear-left corner. Assess at normal size before zooming.
3. **Roof-off oblique/near-plan capture**, from a camera that shows the whole floor and actual furniture. Must demonstrate a compact connected service arrangement, two useful dining tables, one sink, both woks, and customer/staff circulation. This is diagnostic evidence, not a substitute for the enclosed image.
4. **Close front cabinet view**, sufficient to judge duck scale, hooks/rail, glass, chopping station and adjacent serving objects. The focal asset must survive scrutiny.
5. **Reverse interior view toward the entrance**, to expose the customer/staff interface and the underside of the shopfront/ceiling. This guards against a convincing front image concealing an unresolved interior.
