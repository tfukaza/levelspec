## Three highest-impact corrections

1. **Rebuild the lighting and material hierarchy around the food display and hot kitchen.** In the target, the two amber display lamps make the hanging birds and metal frame the foreground focal point; the back oven glows, the hood catches warm edge light, and the dark floor still has readable tile joints and reflected pools. R13 has a pale beige background, near-black floor, mostly neutral lighting, cold gray steel and no convincing lit cooking area. Its interior shows broad ceiling light patches while food and working surfaces remain dull. Correct the display lamp positions and color, give the stove and oven a restrained visible hot source, use warm metal roughness/highlight variation, and retain enough bounced illumination to read the floor. Check the uncut storefront too: clipping changes alone cannot repair its flat lighting. Completion criterion: at thumbnail scale the display is the clear warm foreground focus, the stove is a secondary lit focus, and the walking aisle remains visibly tiled rather than disappearing into black.

2. **Replace the generic rear kitchen silhouettes with the target's recognizable equipment group.** The target has a deep sloped extraction hood with a strongly ribbed intake, a substantial double-wok range with knobs and lower compartments, a rounded roast oven with a bright fire aperture, an exposed sink, and hanging utensils and pipe runs. R13 reads as a thin black horizontal hood, two almost-black bowls, and a dark cylindrical barrel. The back wall is predominantly empty tile. Model a broad sloped hood with visible intake fins, articulate the range front, and give the oven a readable door/aperture and rim. Add the limited utensil rail and utility runs that bind these appliances together. This changes silhouette and depth, not just texture. Completion criterion: the cook line is immediately identifiable in both the cutaway and doorway view without relying on the presence of two woks.

3. **Recompose the stock and work surfaces into dense, varied, occupied stations.** The target has irregular clusters of cartons, tins, dishes and bamboo stacks on the back-left shelf, low central open storage packed with bowls, smaller metal preparation surfaces, and a tightly layered display counter with tray food, dishes, chopping block, register and bags. R13's central island presents a long largely bare wooden slab, back-left shelves have large visibly empty bays, right-side storage becomes long repeated bands, and condiment rows are mechanically uniform. Keep clear circulation, but replace empty shelf planes with stock clusters of visibly different heights and sizes; shorten the apparent uninterrupted bench using two or three distinct preparation/storage stations; give the foreground food trays and dish stacks more visual mass. The target's left wall also has a fan, bright tube and substantial menu/paper cluster, whereas R13's large plain tile field strongly advertises missing density. Completion criterion: after removing the obstructing header, the foreground display, central preparation group and rear stocked shelf all hold their own silhouette and detail at the same image scale, without repeated identical rows becoming the dominant motif.

## Remaining visual observations

The current bird bodies are substantial and recognizable, but the interior crop makes them oversized in the composition, and their subdued brown highlights do not provide the target's glazed amber emphasis. The target uses fewer, more closely grouped birds within a compact counter composition. Camera and display proportions should be tuned together after lighting; do not infer a required bird count from this difference.

Target wear is concentrated around actual use: chipped teal paint, grease near cooking equipment, dirty grout, worn counter edges and wet-looking floor highlights. R13 has some convincing aged paint, but the tiled walls remain broadly uniform and the exterior apron reads as large dark slabs. Merely increasing overall noise would not reproduce this material logic.

The target's wall tops and masonry have physical thickness and uneven small-scale relief; the current cutaway has very thin-looking top edges. This is a secondary silhouette gap, not a reason to ignore the three corrections above.

No acceptance is implied by fixture counts, shader compilation, build success, or current model completeness. Re-render all three actual views after corrections and compare them against this same frozen target. A successful cutaway must be accompanied by an improved uncut storefront/interior so that presentation isolation does not conceal unresolved material and lighting problems.
