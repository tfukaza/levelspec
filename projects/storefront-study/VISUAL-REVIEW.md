# Required visual review contract

The user requires every visual review to identify at least one concrete area for improvement. An acceptance statement alone does not satisfy the review.

Inspect the active reference first, then actual frozen model captures. The active inspiration set is `concepts/industrial-daylight/{grocer,greengrocer,roast}.png`, generated from scratch without screenshot inputs at the user's request. These explore new layouts and 1990s industrial anime atmosphere, including blue shadows, warm practical lights and accumulated architecture. Blue is a palette direction, not a requirement for night or dusk; maintain clear daylight readability. They are concept art, not model screenshots or dimensioned plans. Earlier screenshot paintovers and original photographs remain historical context. Separate atmosphere/material progress from unresolved layout differences; do not claim exact fidelity to these invented spaces.

For every review:

1. Produce a reference/render comparison and inspect the full images.
2. Judge composition, occupied volume, density, material scale, grime, color, light, cast shadows and contact shading. Equipment counts and passing tests do not establish visual quality.
3. Identify at least one evidenced area for improvement: name the object or image region, describe the visible discrepancy, explain its impact, and recommend a concrete change. Prioritize the most consequential gaps. Do not invent defects to fill a quota.
4. State whether the current model matches the visual brief, with unresolved gaps. Do not turn a conspicuous mismatch into optional polish merely because geometry fits.
5. Distinguish model defects from camera, clipping or capture defects; both require correction when they prevent a meaningful comparison.
6. Record the inspected revision and image paths. Separate functional/circulation evidence from visual acceptance.

Use a fresh visual reviewer after each iteration, as requested. Supply the user's brief, reference and actual captures without instructing the reviewer to accept a particular fix or limiting attention to the author's latest change.
