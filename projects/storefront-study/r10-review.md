## Latest duck-shop correction: partially successful

The two woks are unmistakable in the cutaway and visible below the hood in the interior capture. There is one identifiable open sink beside the roaster, rather than repeated washing fixtures. Two tables and four stools make the eat-in function clear, and the register is visibly grounded on the entrance end of the front counter. These are meaningful improvements to the user's specific complaint.

The kitchen still reads oversized in the cutaway. A large central open area separates the front counter, short wall prep bench and far-back cooker. The lightweight rail beside the dining route does little to establish a compact work zone. This is not a demand to fill circulation with props. A shallow prep/storage peninsula on the kitchen side of that divider would give the space a useful work surface, connect preparation to service, and make the working aisle read deliberately bounded. Preserve the front staff crossover and the dining aisle. Approximately 0.45–0.5 m of counter depth is a design suggestion for testing, not a measured fact from the photo. Alternatively, a deliberate rear pantry boundary could shorten the working depth. A layout/circulation reviewer should assess the chosen correction against the actual body envelopes.

The frontal view alone conceals much of this issue. Present the interior or cutaway alongside the frontage when reporting the kitchen correction. Do not claim a complete functional layout merely because two woks and a sink exist.

## Shelves and checkout

The thicker timber decks and predominantly wooden wall banks now communicate the requested construction. The open timber central unit is visually distinct from the solid-backed wall storage, while the entrance and rear metal racks retain useful variety. The new shelf-scale patina no longer produces conspicuous architectural-size peeling islands on the visible steel racks. The frontage capture cannot establish millimeter-scale wear quality; that remains a close-up/material-test question.

The provisions checkout now has a real register visible in the cutaway. Its orientation and exact staff access are separate geometric checks; the frontal scale partially occludes it. The stocked wall and central banks produce readable layers into the room. Preserve this depth and the usable passage rather than filling every dark floor patch to simulate density.

## Remaining art gaps

1. **Floors are the largest newly exposed material gap.** The roast floor is a broad dark red field, and the other interiors read as nearly unpatterned dark planes. In the concepts, tile divisions, rubbed traffic paths and contact grime provide scale and age without obstructing movement. Apply restrained physical-scale floor detail rather than adding more random objects.
2. **Tile weathering is too broad and cloudy.** Especially in the roast interior, large brown-black mottles and a continuous horizontal band dominate the wall. The concepts instead emphasize dirty grout, chipped edges and localized deposits around equipment. This is an art refinement, not a claim that all real restaurant walls must have the concept's extreme grime.
3. **Produce composition remains conspicuously regular.** Four identical saturated red lamps, thin regularly spaced hanging banana groups and discrete tray-sized heaps contrast with the reference's fewer varied shades and larger overlapping leafy masses. Front crate colors are now useful and coherent per crate; retain them. The external produce-asset work should address the organic silhouette rather than replacing it with undifferentiated noise.
4. **Light does not yet support the focal points.** Broad ceiling pools and even illumination make the fixtures and duck case feel less tactile than the research concepts. Modest warm local pools at the case, till and produce could improve this without turning the whole shop orange or making the deep aisles unreadable.
5. **Shared facade wear remains front-loaded in the wrong places.** Large fascia peel patches dominate while lower frame joints, threshold contacts and awning edges are relatively clean. More large patches would worsen this. This is separate from the dedicated shelf material and should not undo that correction.

## Assessment

Accept the thicker predominantly timber shelf direction and the visible one-sink/two-wok/eat-in additions. Request a further kitchen-footprint/composition adjustment before calling the latest duck-shop complaint fully resolved. The current captures show a useful procedural interpretation, not close visual equivalence to the richer concept art. Floor detail, localized wear, organic produce silhouette and focal light remain the main broader art limitations. No production source was edited for this review; body clearance and navigation are not certified here.
